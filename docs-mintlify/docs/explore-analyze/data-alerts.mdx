---
title: Data alerts
description: Notify only when a value on a dashboard crosses a threshold, changes by a percentage, or falls outside its normal range.
---

<Warning>

Data alerts are currently in preview, and their capabilities may still change.
Reach out to the [Cube support team](/admin/account-billing/support) to activate
this feature for your account.

</Warning>

<Info>

Data alerts are available wherever [scheduled refreshes][ref-scheduled-refreshes]
are: on [Premium and Enterprise plans](https://cube.dev/pricing), for users with
at least the [Explorer][ref-roles] role and Edit or Manage permission on the
workbook.

</Info>

A data alert turns a scheduled refresh into a watch on one number. Without an
alert, a schedule with a [notification][ref-notifications] sends it after every
run. With one, each run checks a value on the dashboard, and the notification
goes out only when that value meets the condition you set — for example, when
the orders count goes above 1,000, when revenue drops by 10% or more from the
previous month, or when this week's sign-ups look unusual.

An alert belongs to one schedule. The schedule's frequency is how often the
condition is checked, and its notification — email recipients or a Slack
channel — is who hears about it. The dashboard is still refreshed on every
run, whether the alert fires or not.

## Creating a data alert

1. Open the [scheduled refreshes sidebar][ref-accessing] of a published
   dashboard and click **New scheduled refresh**, or edit an existing
   schedule.
2. Click **Add alert condition**. An **Alert condition** card opens above
   the notification.
3. In **Chart**, pick the chart or KPI to watch. The list holds every chart and
   KPI widget on the published dashboard, including those inside grid, stack,
   and tabs containers. A chart that can't carry an alert is listed but
   disabled; hover it to see why (see
   [Which charts support which rule](#which-charts-support-which-rule)).
4. In **Measure**, pick the value to read. The chart's first measure is
   selected for you. **Measure** and **Condition** stay disabled until you pick
   a chart.
5. Under **Condition**, choose the [rule type](#rule-types) and fill in its
   fields.
6. Click **Add notification** and choose email recipients or a Slack channel.
   An alert needs somewhere to send its notification, so **Save** stays
   disabled until it has one.
7. Click **Save**.

While something is missing, the dialog footer says what it is — for example,
"Enter the value to compare with." or "An alert needs a notification: add
recipients or a Slack channel."

On a dashboard with no charts, the card reads "This dashboard has no charts to
check." and the footer reads "This dashboard has no charts to check. Remove the
alert condition to save." Click the card's ✕ to save the schedule without an
alert.

You can also create an alert from the [Scheduled refreshes][ref-all-schedules]
tab of the **Scheduled** page. Its **New scheduled refresh** dialog shows the
**Add alert condition** button from the start, disabled with "Select a
dashboard to add an alert condition." until you pick a **Dashboard**. After
that, it works as it does in the sidebar.

### From a chart's menu {#from-a-charts-menu}

To watch a particular chart, open that chart or KPI's menu (`⋮`) on the
published dashboard and choose **Create alert**. The **New scheduled refresh**
dialog opens with the **Alert condition** card already open, that chart in
**Chart**, and its first measure in **Measure**; continue from step 5 of the
list above. Everything outside the card starts as it does for **New scheduled
refresh** in the sidebar. When you save, the dialog closes with "Scheduled
refresh created", and the new schedule is listed in the
[scheduled refreshes sidebar][ref-accessing].

**Create alert** is offered only to people who can create schedules on the
dashboard (see the note at the top of this page), and never on an
[embedded dashboard][ref-embedding], published or in
[Creator Mode][ref-creator-mode].

In the [dashboard builder][ref-workbooks], a chart's menu offers **Create
alert** too. An alert reads the published dashboard, so the item is enabled
only on a chart that is in the published version. On a chart added since the
last publish, or on every chart of a dashboard that has never been published,
it is disabled, and hovering it shows "Publish the dashboard to create an alert
on this chart." Once you publish, the item is enabled.

If the chart can't carry an alert — it is split by a dimension, or Cube can't
read its query — the dialog still opens on it, disables every rule type, shows
the reason under them, and keeps **Save** disabled with, for example, "This
chart has no single value to check. Choose another chart." Pick another chart
in **Chart** to continue.

## Rule types

### Threshold

The condition is met when the measure **is above**, **is below**, or **is equal
to** the number you enter in **Value**. **Is equal to** compares exactly, so it
suits whole-number measures such as counts.

### % change

The condition is met when the measure **increases by ≥**, **decreases by ≥**,
or **changes by ≥** (in either direction) the percentage you enter, comparing
the chart's last complete period with the period before it.

When the previous period's value is 0 there is no percentage to compute, so the
check can't run and nothing is sent.

### Anomaly

The condition is met when the last complete period falls outside the normal
range of the periods before it. The normal range is their median ± 3 × the
median absolute deviation. If every earlier period has the same value, the range
is that value alone, and any other value is outside it.

The rule needs at least 8 complete periods before the latest one. Until the
chart has them, the check can't run and nothing is sent.

### Which charts support which rule

The dialog offers a chart and a rule type only where the chart has the value it
reads, and says why when it doesn't:

- A chart or KPI **without a time dimension** supports **Threshold** only. **%
  change** and **Anomaly** compare periods, so they are disabled, and the
  reason shows under the rule types.
- A chart **split by a dimension** besides its time dimension — a bar chart by
  category, or a line chart with one line per status — has no single value to
  check, so it is disabled in the **Chart** list. Its tooltip says why, for
  example "“Orders by month and status” is split by Status, so it has no single
  value to check. Pick a KPI or a chart without a breakdown." A chart with
  several measures and no breakdown is fine: you pick one measure.
- A chart whose query Cube can't read, such as a Python analysis or a chart
  built from SQL, is disabled in the **Chart** list too.

If the chart an alert already watches can no longer carry one — for example, it
is now split by a dimension — **Edit** keeps that chart in **Chart**, disables
every rule type, and shows the reason under them.

## What an alert checks

**The data the notification shows.** The alert reads the chart with the
schedule's [control values][ref-control-values], and with the dashboard's
published default for every control the schedule leaves on **Use default** —
the same data the notification's screenshot shows. For example, if the
dashboard's Status filter defaults to completed orders and the schedule sets it
to **Clear**, the alert reads the orders of every status. The dialog, every
alert email, and every Slack message say so: "Checked with the dashboard's
default filters and this schedule's saved filters." If you republish the
dashboard or change a default, the next check uses the new version.

**The last complete period.** On a chart with a time dimension, every rule reads
the last _complete_ period: the period still in progress, and any later periods
the data holds, are skipped. A monthly chart checked on September 15 reads
August. Periods follow the granularity the chart is drawn with, so a
[time granularity switcher][ref-time-granularity] that changes it changes the
period the alert reads. On a chart without a time dimension, the alert reads its
single value.

**The schedule creator's access.** The check runs once per run, with the data
access of the person who created the schedule.

## When an alert notifies

An alert notifies when its condition **starts** to hold, and again only after it
has stopped holding and then starts again. While the condition keeps holding,
later runs send nothing, so a value that stays above its threshold for a week
sends one notification, not seven. There is no notification when the condition
stops holding.

A notification that reaches no one — for example, when the only recipient is a
group with no members — doesn't count as sent: the next run that finds the
condition met tries again.

**Run now** checks against the same history as a scheduled run: pressing it
twice while the condition holds notifies once.

The alert starts fresh — as if its condition had never held, with its card
back to "Last check: not run yet" — when you turn the schedule off, edit the
alert's condition, or change the schedule's
[control values][ref-control-values]: the next check notifies if the condition
holds on what it now reads. Changing only the schedule's time keeps the
history. A check that can't run leaves the history as it was.

A run whose alert does not fire is a plain refresh: no screenshot is generated,
no [AI summary][ref-ai-summary] is written, and nothing is sent.

## The notification

A fired alert sends the schedule's usual notification — the dashboard link and
screenshot, and the [AI summary][ref-ai-summary] if it is turned on — led by
what the alert found:

- **Email** — the subject is `Alert: <chart> on <dashboard>`. The message names
  the condition, the chart, and the value read: the period it read, plus the
  previous period and the change for **% change**, or the normal range for
  **Anomaly**.
- **Slack** — the message opens with the same `Alert: <chart> on <dashboard>`
  line, followed by what the alert read, for example "Count increased by 2.5%
  from 157 in 2026-08 to 161 in 2026-09 (alert at 1% or more)."

Both state that the check used the dashboard's default filters and the
schedule's saved filters, and that you will be notified again only after the
condition stops holding.

<Info>

Because the check runs with the schedule creator's access, the values it read
appear only in the creator's email and in the Slack message. Every other email
recipient gets the condition and the chart, a note that the values are not shown
because the check ran with the schedule creator's access, and a screenshot of
the dashboard with their own access.

</Info>

## Following an alert in the sidebar

In the scheduled refreshes sidebar, a schedule with an alert carries an
**Alert** badge beside its schedule and a summary of its rule, for example
"Condition: Count is above 1,000 · Orders count". On the
[Scheduled refreshes][ref-all-schedules] tab, the same summary, without
"Condition:", fills the **Condition** column.

The card's last line shows the last check. Before the first one it reads "Last
check: not run yet"; afterwards it says how long ago the check ran, what it
read, and what came of it, for example "Last check: 3 minutes ago, Count
increased by 2.7%, notified". What it read depends on the rule:

- **Threshold** — the value, for example "Count was 20,003".
- **% change** — the change, for example "Count increased by 2.7%" or "Count
  decreased by 4%".
- **Anomaly** — "Count outside its normal range" or "Count within its normal
  range".

What came of it is one of these:

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| notified | The condition started to hold and the notification was sent. |
| still met, not notified again | The condition was already holding at the previous check. |
| met, nothing sent | The condition started to hold, but the notification reached no one. |
| not met | Nothing was sent. |
| could not check | The check couldn't run. Nothing was sent. |

How long ago is given in its largest unit, as for the next run: "less than a
minute ago", "3 minutes ago", "1 hour ago", or "2 days ago".

Hovering the line shows it whole and, under it, the whole check: when it ran,
in your timezone and naming it, the value it read, and, for **% change**, the
previous period and the change, for example "Last check Oct 17, 2026, 3:08 AM
GMT+1: Count 339 (2026-09). Previous: 330 (2026-08), +2.7%. Condition not
met." For **Anomaly**, it names the normal range, for example "Normal range: 8
– 14." For a check that couldn't run, it says why. The **Last check** column
of the [Scheduled refreshes][ref-all-schedules] tab shows the same line,
without "Last check:", and the same text on hover.

The values are shown only to the schedule's creator. Anyone else sees how long
ago the check ran and what came of it, for example "Last check: 1 minute ago,
still met, not notified again", and on hover "values not shown, as it ran with
the access of the schedule's creator."

### When a check can't run

A check that can't run sends nothing and doesn't change whether the alert
counts as holding. Hovering the last-check line shows why, for example:

- The chart is no longer on the dashboard, or the measure is no longer in its
  query.
- The chart is now split by a dimension.
- The chart returned no rows, or there is no complete period yet.
- An **Anomaly** rule has too few complete periods, or a **% change** rule's
  previous period was 0.

When the chart or measure is gone, **Edit** shows the alert with the missing
field flagged, for example "Count is no longer in “Orders by month”. Pick
another measure."

## Editing, duplicating, and deleting

Edit a schedule to change or remove its alert: click the ✕ (**Remove alert
condition**) on the **Alert condition** card to make it a plain scheduled
refresh again. [Duplicating a schedule][ref-duplicate] copies its alert, and
deleting a schedule deletes its alert.

If data alerts are turned off for your account, schedules keep their alerts but
send nothing while they do. Editing such a schedule shows only the **Alert
condition** switch; turn it off to remove the alert. When data alerts are turned
back on, the alerts resume where they left off.
